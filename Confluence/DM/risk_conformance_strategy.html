Starting on Conformance — A Working Strategy for Risk

Companion to Three Models for Risk Data — how we propose to begin the work, within Risk, without waiting for enterprise

This companion note proposes a practical starting point. The conceptual document explains what conformance is and why it matters. This one addresses how we begin — given the apps we have today, the reports they produce, and the analytics work already underway. The intent is to start within Risk, make progress on what is locally actionable, and contribute back to the enterprise canonical work as it matures.

SECTION 1

Where we are starting from

The analytical work is valuable, but it sits on data that has not yet been fully conformed.

The honest picture

We are building analytical models — star schemas, dashboards, semantic layers — on top of the data that risk applications produce today as reports and distributed outputs. This work is valuable: it demonstrates capability, surfaces real business questions, and gives us a vehicle for the broader conversation.

At the same time, the source reports were produced without an explicit conformance discipline applied to the inputs the apps consumed. The apps each made their own choices about where to source attributes, how to combine them, and how to handle gaps and conflicts. Some of those choices were documented; many were not. The analytics inherit those limitations.

What this means in practice

  • Analytics built today on the current data are reliable within the boundary of one app's report. They show what that app produced, faithfully.
  • Where analytics combine outputs across apps — for example, comparing exposures from CCR with valuations from VS — results are usable for direction, but precision should not be claimed until the underlying conformance work has been done.
  • Business consumers should be aware of this distinction. The analytical capability is real; full cross-app trust is a separate, ongoing piece of work.
The two tracks proceed in parallel. Analytical work does not need to wait for conformance. Conformance work does not need to wait for analytics. As the conformance work matures in the source apps, the analytics built on top become progressively more trustworthy. Both tracks are needed; neither alone is sufficient.
SECTION 2

The working strategy — two waves within Risk

Start where the conversation is tightest. Expand outward as understanding builds.

Why bottom-up, scoped to Risk

Risk has many product groups, each containing several applications. Within each group, the apps share domain language, data sources, and business consumers. Conversations about "what does Counterparty really mean here" are most tractable inside a group. We propose starting there, then expanding outward.

Enterprise canonical models for cross-domain entities — Trade, Security Master, Party Master, Account — are being worked on in parallel by the enterprise data team. Risk contributes to that work but does not pause its own progress while it matures.

Diagram

Figure 1 — Two-wave approach: each Risk product group does its own conformance work first, then groups compare across, with enterprise canonical work proceeding in parallel.

Why this order

  • Within a group, the conversation is easier. Apps in Credit Risk share more context with each other than with apps in Liquidity. Conformance discussions converge faster among teams with shared business language.
  • Each group can move at its own pace. No group has to wait for another to start. CCR / Counterparty Risk is the natural early mover because the analytics POC is already running there.
  • Cross-group work has a foundation to land on. When the risk-wide comparison happens in Wave 2, each group brings a coherent picture rather than ad-hoc per-app submissions.
SECTION 3

What each product group does

A small number of concrete steps. The spreadsheet you already have starts most of this.

The four steps within a group

StepWhat it producesWho does it
1. Classify report attributes Each app maps the attributes in its reports to entities (Counterparty, Security, Trade, Agreement, Position, etc.). Surfaces which entities each app depends on. Each app team, individually
2. Declare the sourcing contract For each entity, the app declares its sources, fallback rules, distribution platform, schedule, protocol, and transformations — per the conformance reference doc. Each app team, individually
3. Compare across the group The group's apps share their classifications and contracts. Where the same entity is constructed differently across apps, the gaps are surfaced and discussed. Group working session, with risk data team facilitation
4. Decide and document For entities the group can settle internally, the conformed version is agreed. For entities that span groups or require enterprise input, the decision is deferred and documented. Group leads, with risk data team

What is already in place

  • A spreadsheet template exists, capturing source declarations from each app. Several apps have started populating it.
  • The conceptual framework (the four-page reference doc) gives a shared vocabulary for the discussion.
  • The CCR / Collateral analytics POC provides a concrete example of what an analytical output looks like when the underlying conformance work is partial — useful as a reference point for the wider conversation.

What we propose adding

  • A simple entity classification list per app — not exhaustive, just the entities the app's reports actually touch.
  • Group-level working sessions to compare and converge. Once per group, then ongoing as needed.
  • A risk-wide register of conformed entities and deferrals — lightweight, maintained by the risk data team.
The aim of Wave 1 is clarity, not perfection. A group has succeeded when each of its apps can describe, in writing, where its data comes from and what entities its outputs depend on — and when the group has a shared picture of where the conformance gaps are. Closing the gaps is the next phase. Naming them is the starting point.
SECTION 4

Where we propose to start

No forced timeline. Start where the conversation is already underway.

Suggested first movers

The product groups most ready to begin are the ones where the analytical conversation is already happening. Counterparty Risk and Credit Risk are the natural early movers — the CCR / Collateral POC is running, business consumers are engaged, and the conformance gaps in the underlying data are already visible through the analytical work. Starting Wave 1 there builds momentum and produces a concrete reference for other groups to see.

Other groups (Market Risk, Valuation Services, Funding, Finance, Liquidity) can begin when they are ready. We are not proposing a forced sequence — just suggesting that early movers go where the conversation is most concrete.

What success looks like in six months

  • Each participating group has completed the four steps for the entities most relevant to its apps.
  • The risk-wide register has its first entries — a small number of entities with conformed definitions, and a clear list of deferrals.
  • The analytical work in CCR / Collateral has progressively more conformed data underneath it, and the gap between "analytics demonstrates capability" and "analytics is trusted for cross-app use" is visibly narrowing.
  • The conversation with enterprise on cross-domain entities (Trade, Security Master, Party Master) is being informed by what Risk has discovered in its own work.
One closing point. Conformance is a discipline, not a project. It is the work of declaring, explicitly, what each piece of data means and where it came from. Once started, it accumulates. The first entities conformed make the next ones easier. The cost is mostly upfront; the value compounds.
