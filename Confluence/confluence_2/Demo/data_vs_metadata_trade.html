What is metadata, really?
Look at this data. Try to make sense of it. Now look again — with metadata.
Step 1 Here's some data. It's a few rows from a trade table. Looks fine.

Step 2 Now try to use it. Hold on. There are questions you can't answer.
cpty_cd— Bloomberg? Internal code? LEI? notional— what currency? mtm— calculated by whom? As of when? ns— "netting set" or "non-settled"? asof— close-of-business in which timezone? Can I join this with App B's data?
Metadata!
The data about the data — what each field means, where it came from, what unit it's in.

So why bother? What does metadata unlock?
Once every column knows what it is, where it came from, and how it relates to other data — a lot becomes possible.
🧩
Entity Conformity
The big one. Two apps both have a column called cpty_cd. Without metadata, you assume they mean the same thing. They don't.
App A · xvala · cpty_cd = "JPMC"
↳ source: Veritas · scheme: internal_code
App B · cmos · cpty_cd = "037833100"
↳ source: Bloomberg · scheme: cusip
Same column name. Different worlds.
Metadata is what makes conformity possible — you can't conform what you can't describe.
⚙️
Automation
If every column declares its type, nullability, FK, and grain — pipelines, DQ checks, and lineage tracking write themselves.
Example: a column tagged PII = yes automatically gets masked in dev environments. No human asked.
🤖
AI & Analytics
Models, LLMs, and BI tools need context. "What does mtm mean?" is answerable when the metadata says so.
Example: an LLM asked "show me PFE exposure to JPMC" can find the right table — because the metadata says x-measure = PFE, refersTo = counterparty.
💡 The lightbulb: Data is the noun. Metadata is everything that tells you what kind of noun it is.
Without metadata, every consumer of your data does detective work. With metadata, they just use it.
