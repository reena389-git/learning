Business Intake — Tell us what you need

How to describe a use case so the data team can build it.

This is normally a conversation. In a mature data practice, a Data Product Owner would sit with you and walk through these questions in person — asking, listening, capturing your answers. We don't yet have that role at TD, so we're asking you to write down the answers yourself. Think of this as a structured conversation rather than a form to complete.

What this intake is for

The Business Intake captures your world — what you do today, what you want to be able to do, and what's hard about it — in a way that lets the data team translate it into a published, governed data product in the risk_warehouse.

Don't worry about technical vocabulary. Describe what you need in your own words. The data team will translate "I want to see exposure by counterparty broken down by direct, indirect, and CCR" into entities, measures, contracts, and so on. Your job is to describe the business need clearly and completely.

What it takes

Seven short sections, around 1–2 pages of content when complete. The intake template comes pre-filled with a worked example so you can see what a completed intake looks like before you fill in your own.

Tip. If you find yourself stuck, write what you would say if someone asked you the question in a meeting. Plain language. Don't try to be precise or technical — that's the data team's job.

What happens after you submit

Your intake goes to the data team and the relevant application teams. Within a couple of days you'll have a follow-up conversation to clarify the trickier parts. Your intake is the input to the work that follows:

  • Entities you name (counterparty, agreement, position…) become entries in the canonical data model
  • Measures you describe (exposure, limits, breaches…) inform the dimensional model
  • Reports you currently consume help us understand what the published view needs to support
  • Validation rules you describe become the data quality tests on the published product

Once the data product is built and published in the risk_warehouse, you'll be able to consume it through dashboards, BI tools, and ad-hoc query. The data team will let you know when it's ready.

The intake — pre-filled with the worked example

This is what the fillable form looks like. The yellow-shaded fields show the worked example — what a business person from Counterparty Credit Risk reporting might write.

1 Who you are
Warm-up question. The point of contact and the decision-maker.
Your name and team
Example fill-in
Name: [Business person filling in the intake]
Team: Counterparty Credit Risk Reporting
Role: Risk Reporting Lead
Email: [Author email]
Who else needs to be in the conversation?
The decision-maker if it's not you. Any other consumers of the eventual data product.
Example fill-in
Decision-maker: Head of Counterparty Credit Risk
Other consumers: Concentration risk team, Limit management team, Credit committee secretariat
2 What you're trying to do
One paragraph. The business outcome you're chasing, in your own words.
In plain language, what do you want to be able to do?
Think of how you'd describe this to a colleague over coffee. What question are you trying to answer? What decisions does this support?
Example fill-in
I want to see our total exposure to each counterparty, every business day, so I can monitor concentration risk and identify counterparties where our exposure is approaching limit. Today I cobble this together from three or four different reports and Excel files and it takes me half a day. By the time I have a number, it's stale and my limit team has already asked twice. I need this to be a single, reliable view that I can trust without manually reconciling it every morning.
What decisions does this support?
Who acts on this information? What do they do with it?
Example fill-in
The credit committee uses it for weekly concentration review. The limit management team uses it daily to flag counterparties approaching threshold. The relationship managers use it ad-hoc when a client asks about their overall position with us. Regulatory reporting uses similar numbers for BCBS 239 and large-exposure submissions but those go through a separate workflow today.
3 How you do this today
The as-is state. What reports you consume, what you do with them, what's hard about it.
What reports or data sources do you use today?
List them. For each: where it comes from, how often you get it, what format.
Report or data sourceWhere it comes fromHow oftenFormat
Direct Exposure Daily Pack Lending Operations Daily, ~6 AM Excel attached to email
Indirect Exposure (Guarantees) Legal & Documentation team Weekly, Mondays PDF + Excel
CCR PFE Pack CCR engine team (Mosaic output) Daily, ~7 AM CSV via shared drive
Counterparty Hierarchy Reference KYC Operations Monthly + ad-hoc updates Excel
What do you do with these reports?
The manual steps you go through. The joins, the lookups, the validations, the spreadsheet gymnastics.
Example fill-in
Every morning I copy the three exposure packs into a master spreadsheet. I VLOOKUP the counterparty IDs across the three sources because they're not always identical — Lending uses one counterparty code and the CCR engine uses a slightly different one for a few legal entities. I roll up subsidiaries to their ultimate parent using the KYC hierarchy file. I convert non-USD exposures to USD using last night's FX rates. Then I check the totals against yesterday's number and flag anything that moved more than 10%. By the time I'm done, it's 10 AM and the credit committee meeting has already started.
What's hard about this?
Where the friction is. What goes wrong. What you wish was different.
Example fill-in
Three things. First, the counterparty IDs not matching across sources — I've had cases where we double-counted or missed a counterparty because two sources disagreed on its ID. Second, the manual roll-up to ultimate parent — this is the kind of thing that should just be done once, centrally, not by me with a VLOOKUP. Third, the time pressure — by the time the numbers are ready, half the people who need them have moved on. I want a view I can open at 7 AM that's already correct.
4 What you need from the new view
The to-be state. What you want to be able to see, slice, and refresh.
What information should show up?
List the things you want to see. Plain language — don't worry about technical names.
Example fill-in
For each counterparty, on each business date, I want to see: • Total exposure (the headline number — direct + indirect + CCR combined) • Direct exposure (broken out) • Indirect exposure (broken out — and ideally I can see which guarantor chain) • CCR line exposure (broken out — base case Potential Future Exposure) • Counterparty's ultimate parent (so I can roll up) • Counterparty's industry / sector (for concentration cuts) • Country of risk
How would you want to slice, filter, or group it?
In plain language — "by counterparty," "by date," "by region," etc.
Example fill-in
• By counterparty (the primary cut) • By ultimate parent (rolling subsidiaries up) • By industry or sector (for concentration analysis) • By country of risk • By date (so I can see the trend over time, or compare today to a month ago) • Filter by exposure threshold (e.g. "show me only counterparties above $50M total exposure")
How often does it need to refresh? By what time?
Once a day? Several times a day? Immediately when something changes? Plain language.
Example fill-in
Daily, end-of-business-day close. Available by 7 AM the next morning at the latest. If it's not available by 8 AM, I need to know — the credit committee meeting is at 9 AM and I can't show up empty-handed.
5 Trust and validation
What rules should always hold. How you'd spot a problem.
What rules should always be true about this data?
Things that, if they weren't true, you'd know something was wrong. Plain language.
Example fill-in
• Total exposure should never be negative • If a counterparty has no positions, no guarantees, and no derivatives, they shouldn't show up at all • The number of counterparties shouldn't change wildly from one day to the next — maybe a 5–10% band is normal • Every counterparty in this view should also exist in our master counterparty list (no orphans) • Total exposure should equal direct + indirect + CCR — these should never disagree
How do you know when something is wrong today?
The gut-check tests you do. What "feels off" looks like.
Example fill-in
The first thing I look at is the top 20 counterparties by exposure. I know roughly what the top 20 should look like — the same big names appear most days. If suddenly there's a counterparty I've never heard of in the top 20, or one of the usual names drops off, something's wrong. The second thing is the total — our total exposure moves slowly, so if it changes by more than 10% day-on-day, I check the data before I report.
6 Sensitivity and constraints
Who should and shouldn't see this. Any regulatory drivers.
Who should NOT see this data?
Any sensitivity concerns you're aware of. If you're not sure, say so — the data team will work it out.
Example fill-in
This is confidential. Should be available to credit risk, limit management, the credit committee, and the relevant relationship managers (when their own clients are involved). Should NOT be available outside Risk — not Front Office sales, not Operations broadly. No PII concerns — counterparties are legal entities, not individuals.
Are there regulatory drivers behind this?
Specific regulations, reporting obligations, audit requirements. If you're not sure, say so.
Example fill-in
Yes. BCBS 239 (risk data aggregation principles) applies. Our large-exposure regulatory submissions use related but separately governed numbers — those go through a different workflow and shouldn't be confused with this view. This view is for internal risk management, not for regulatory submission.
7 Anything else
Catch-all. Anything weighing on you that the structure didn't capture.
Anything else the data team should know?
Constraints, history, things that have gone wrong before, related work, timing pressure, anything.
Example fill-in
Two things. One — there's an ongoing project to clean up the counterparty hierarchy (the parent/subsidiary tree). If that lands before this work, great. If not, we need to use the hierarchy that exists today, even though we know it has gaps for some Asian subsidiaries. Two — there's a related view the market risk team wants for stressed exposure (PFE under shock scenarios). They're a separate use case but if there's a way to build this so that stressed PFE can plug in later, that would help.

What happens next

Submit the filled-in intake. The data team will reach out within a couple of business days to walk through it with you. Expect questions about:

  • Anything the data team needs help understanding from your description
  • Whether entities you've named already exist in the canonical model or need to be built
  • Whether the cadence and trust rules you described are deliverable as-is or need adjustment
  • Timeline expectations
