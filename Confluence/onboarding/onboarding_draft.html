Onboarding to the Risk Analytics Environment

How to bring a business use case — or an application's data — into the Databricks-based risk analytics environment. We're just starting; this page describes the path.

Where we are today. The Databricks analytics environment has just been stood up. Risk applications are loading their data into application-specific schemas. The risk_warehouse catalog — the business-facing layer that consumers will query — is not yet in use. This onboarding process is how we get the first business use cases and the first application data products into that catalog.

Choose your starting point

Onboarding takes two different paths depending on who you are. Pick the one that matches you; both lead to the same end state.

Path A

I'm a business / use case owner →

You consume reports today. You want a new analytical view, or you want a report you already use to be reproduced reliably in the analytics environment.

→ Start with the Business Intake.

Path B

I'm an application / data product owner →

You own a risk application generating data (positions, exposures, collateral, etc.). You're being asked to publish a data product into the analytics environment.

→ Start with the Data Product Canvas.

The path end-to-end (both routes converge)

Both streams produce the same final artefact: a certified data product in the risk_warehouse catalog, published with a v8 data contract. The business stream and the application stream meet in the middle, where the data product canvas gets filled in.

Business — Step 1

1Business Intake

The use case owner describes what they need, what they consume today, and what's hard about it.

  • Use case statement (what question are you trying to answer?)
  • Today's reports inventory (what you consume now)
  • Data sources inventory (where it comes from)
  • Entities and measures involved
  • Today's validation work
  • Gaps and friction
→ Open the Business Intake Template (Excel / SharePoint)

Application / Data Team — Step 3

3Build & Publish

The data product is built and published into the risk_warehouse catalog, backed by:

  • A v8 data contract (YAML in Git)
  • CDM entities (as they get built — incremental over time)
  • Dimensional model / Metric View
  • Unity Catalog tags, classification, lineage
  • Quality constraints and monitoring tests

Path A: Business Intake

The business intake captures what you consume today, what you want to be able to do, and what's hard about it. It's a structured way of describing your world so the data team can translate it into entities, measures, and a published contract.

What you need to do

  1. Open the intake template (link below) — comes pre-filled with the worked example
  2. Replace the example content with your own across the tabs
  3. Submit / share with the data team
  4. Expect a follow-up conversation to clarify entities and measures

What we'll do with your intake

The data team takes your intake and uses it as the input to the Data Product Canvas (see Path B). Entities you name go into the CDM; measures you describe go into the dimensional model; reports you currently use inform what the published data product needs to support.

Path B: Data Product Canvas

The Data Product Canvas is the application / data team's design template. It's a one-page visual with eight blocks that capture everything about a data product: what it is, where it comes from, what it exposes, who consumes it. Each block on the canvas maps to one or more sections of the v8 data contract.

Industry grounding. The Data Product Canvas is the INNOQ Data Product Canvas (CC BY 4.0), used widely in the data mesh community. We've extended it with TD's nine contract sections to bridge the design-time canvas to the build-time contract.

How the canvas maps to TD's v8 contract sections

Canvas BlockWhat goes in itv8 Contract Section
1. Domain Which TD risk LOB owns this product. e.g. credit_risk Section 1: x-producer_domain
2. Data Product Name Stable ID + display name. e.g. exposure_by_counterparty Section 1: id, name
3. Consumer & Use Case Who needs this and what for. Direct input from Business Intake. Section 2: description.purpose
4. Data Contract Output interface — schema, grain, measures, SLA, quality, change policy Sections 4 (Schema), 5 (SLA), 6 (Quality), 7 (Change Policy)
5. Sources Input ports — upstream apps, tables, reference data Section 10 (Deferred): x-reference_dependencies, x-domain_entities
6. Data Product Architecture Internals — how the data is ingested, transformed, stored Not in contract; documented in Confluence product page (linked from references)
7. Ubiquitous Language Domain terminology shared between business and tech Section 4: schema.properties[].description
8. Classification foundational · derived · consumer_aligned (canvas) plus sensitivity tier Section 1: x-archetype · Section 8: x-classification

Note: Blocks 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8 land directly in the v8 contract. Block 5 is captured today in description / Confluence and will move into the deferred stub sections of the YAML as those upstream artefacts (Domain Model, Reference Data registry) get built. Block 6 is documented separately as a Confluence product page.
