How to read this page

The colours here are your own colours — the same legend your spreadsheet uses. Every attribute of every record belongs to one of these families:

Position Core — identity: who, where, when, what kind
Sub-domain details — extra facts only this position type has
Product / Instrument — what financial thing it is
Metrics — every number measured about the position

The one trick to watch: identifiers repeat. The swap's position id POS-TRD-000123 appears on the position record and on every one of its metric rows — that repetition is the join. Tap or hover any code like that and every place it appears on this page lights up yellow. That is how you see the connections.

The composite key — say it like a sentence
A position is uniquely named by: Position Identifier + System Name + Position Date + Snap Type. In words: "Position 000123, as known by Murex, as it stood at end-of-day on 31 July 2026." Every metric row carries this whole sentence again, plus which leg and which measurement. So one metric row reads: "For that same position, on the fixed leg, the present value, in USD, was −8,912,400 — calculated by the FO pricer at 18:42."
Sign convention used on this page
Positive = good for the bank (money we will receive / value we own). Negative = money we will pay / value we owe. Deposits are naturally negative-ish (the bank owes the client their money back); loans are naturally positive (the client owes us). Illustrative numbers, rounded.

Product vs Instrument — the child version
Product = the species ("an interest-rate swap", "a corporate bond"). Instrument = the named individual everyone in the market can hold ("the ABC Corp 4.25% 2031 bond, ISIN US000ABC1234"). A bond is fungible — my bond and your bond are interchangeable — so it gets an instrument id. An OTC swap is a private contract between two parties, so it has a product type but no instrument id. That is why in your LDM the Instruments box hangs off Product with a dashed "instrument used by product" line: only some products resolve to instruments.

Anatomy of a Position record

Every position — swap, bond, cash, deposit, facility, drawdown — is stored with the same core spine. Here is what each core attribute means in plain words, before we fill them in six times:

Core attributePlain-words meaning
Position IdentifierThe position's own name. Stable across days — POS-TRD-000123 today is the same swap position as yesterday.
System NameWhich source system's world this id lives in. "000123" in Murex and "000123" in LoanIQ are different things — the pair (id, system) is what's unique.
Position DateThe business date the snapshot describes: 2026-07-31 everywhere on this page.
Position Snap TypeWhich photograph of that day: EOD (official end of day), INTRADAY, FLASH… Same position, same date, can have several snaps.
Book NameThe shelf inside the bank where the position sits (links to Booking Party via the book relationship).
Aggregation TypeIs this row one contract (TRADE) or a rolled-up pile (AGGREGATED — e.g. all settled holdings of one bond in one book)?
Position TypeWhich of the six flavours: TRADE, AGG_FUNGIBLE, CASH_BALANCE, DEPOSIT_ARRANGEMENT, LENDING_FACILITY, LENDING_OUTSTANDING — the abstract subtypes in your LDM.
Position RegionWhere the position is owned/booked (AMER here). Note: pricing region lives on the metric, because the same position can be valued by different regional runs.
Counterparty CodeWho is on the other side. Filled for the swap, deposit, facility, drawdown (CP-88421); empty for the fungible bond pile and the nostro cash.
Instrument IdentifierFilled only when the position is in a market instrument (the bond). Empty for OTC contracts and balances.
Product Type / Product IdentifierThe green boxes: what species of financial thing this is, pointing into the Product domain.

Everything a specific flavour needs beyond this spine goes into its orange sub-domain block — that is the "one core + typed extensions" pattern your Position Concept sheet draws.

3.1 · Trade Position — the interest rate swap (with legs)

The story, like to a child

Maple Auto Parts borrowed money elsewhere at a floating rate and is scared rates will rise. So in Sept 2024 it made a deal with our rates desk: "for 5 years, on $50 million, you pay me the floating SOFR rate, and I pay you a fixed 3.85%." From the bank's side: we pay fixed (Leg 1) and receive float (Leg 2). Rates went up since 2024 — SOFR now earns us ~4.12% while we still only pay 3.85% — so this trade is currently a winner for us: it is worth about +$538k.

3.2 · Agg Fungible Position — the bond pile

The story, like to a child

Our credit desk bought the same ABC Corp bond many times, in many little trades — 3M face here, 2M there, sold 1M back… Listing every trade forever would be silly, because every bond of the same ISIN is identical (fungible). So once trades settle, we just keep score: "book NY-CREDIT-02 holds $10M face of US000ABC1234." That running score is the aggregated position — your "aggregated positions directly sourced, no parent domain" note: it isn't copied from one parent trade, it's a sum.

The two clocks in action — a correction, without deleting anything

The story, like to a child

On the evening of July 31 the pricer said the swap was worth +538,200. Next morning a curve mark was fixed and the rerun said +542,850 for the same July 31 EOD. We never erase the first answer — we write a second row. Both say "about July 31" (same effective time); they differ in when we learned them (inserted time). Ask "what do we now believe July 31 was?" → latest inserted row. Ask "what did the 7pm report show?" → the first row. That is bitemporality, and it is why regulators can replay any report you ever produced.

05

Risk metrics decoded — factor, node, shock size, shock type

Your Risk Metric subtype adds exactly four attributes. Together they form a question, and Metric Value is the answer:

Risk Factor — "what market thing moved?"

A named market driver: a curve (USD-SOFR-OIS), a spread curve (ABC-SEN-USD-SPRD), an FX pair, an equity, a vol surface. The risk engine's dictionary of things that can wiggle.

Term Node — "which point on it?"

Curves aren't one number; they're a shape with pillars (1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 10Y…). Term node says which pillar was bumped. Non-curve factors (spot FX) simply leave it blank. TOTAL rows sum the pillars.

Shock Size — "moved by how much?"

0.0001 = one basis point. 0.01 = 1%. Sensitivities use tiny shocks; stress scenarios use big ones (e.g. 0.02 = +200bp). Same model row shape for both.

Shock Type — "moved in what way?"

ABSOLUTE (add 1bp to the rate) vs RELATIVE (multiply by 1.01); could also encode parallel vs pillar-wise. It tells you how to reproduce the bump.

Read the swap's risk block like a sentence

"Position POS-TRD-000123, whole position (NET): if USD-SOFR-OIS at the 3Y pillar rises by 0.0001 absolute, we gain $10,400." Why is 3Y the big pillar? Because on 2026-07-31 the swap has ~3.1 years left — its sensitivity naturally piles up at the pillar nearest its remaining life. The 2029 maturity is doing the talking.

Why store risk as rows, not columns?
Tomorrow the desk adds a 7Y pillar, or a new inflation curve, or a 300bp stress. As rows, that's just… more rows. As columns ("DV01_3Y", "DV01_5Y"…) every addition is a schema change across every table and report. This single choice is most of why the metric spine survives new products unchanged — and it's the same reason the model could absorb lending fees and LC fees without a redesign.

Where do VaR and counterparty exposure live? They're computed from these building blocks but usually attach at portfolio level — your "Portfolio Level Metrics" data domain hanging off Position Metric Types. Section 06 shows the roll-up.

07

Why the model is shaped this way — the recap

  • One core + typed extensions. Six very different businesses (trading, cash mgmt, lending) land in one queryable spine; each keeps its oddities (facility attrs, depositAccountBalance) in its orange block. New position type = new subtype, not new platform.
  • Positions are snapshots, trades are events. The Trade domain records what happened; the Position domain records what we hold at a date+snap. That's why date and snap type sit inside the key.
  • Facility ≠ Outstanding. Two positions, linked by FAC-2024-0097, so $100M committed and $40M drawn never double-count — the exact trap your concept sheet calls out.
  • Metrics are rows, keyed like sentences. Any engine can add any measurement about any slice (leg!) of any position without schema change — and source + pricing region let competing answers coexist honestly.
  • Two clocks. Effective vs inserted time makes corrections additive and every historical report replayable.
  • Product/Instrument split. Contracts have products; only fungibles resolve to instruments — which is also why agg positions key on instrument while trade positions key on the trade.

The cross-currency leg problem — the meeting's worked example

The debate, like to a child

Recordings 1 & 3 circled one trade shape: a cross-currency swap, where the two legs are in different currencies, so there is no single natural trade-level PV — someone must pick an FX rate to add USD apples to CAD oranges. The model happily stores two leg rows (that's what Leg Type / Leg Identifier are for). The fight is about the adding: if every consumer converts with their own mandated rate (ECB fix for EU reporting, BoC 4pm for Canadian books…), two honest consumers publish two different "official" trade PVs — reg reporting, P&L and trade surveillance stop reconciling.

Meeting direction: source the most granular (leg level, native currency) as the truth; optionally carry a standard reporting-equivalent on each row so simple consumers can just sum; and if a producer also ships its own trade-level number, label it so nobody double counts.

The Leg Identifier attribute (already in the newer model version) is the join point — but a published mapping per producer is what makes "the trade leg and the valuation leg are the same leg" consistently true, which was the closing ask of recording 1.

One delta vector, three costumes — HBase, JSON, Databricks, and where the CDM sits

The idea, like to a child

The same five numbers — our swap's DV01 by tenor — get dressed differently in each system: as column names in HBase, as a nested list in a JSON message, and as plain rows in Databricks. None of those outfits is the model. The CDM is the meaning underneath: one measured number + the attributes that describe it. As long as every costume can be undressed back to that grain, they're all the same data.

Costume 1 — HBase today: the array hides in the column names

In the current store, a delta vector is one "wide row": the row key names the position and date, a column family (say risk) groups the metric family, and each tenor is a dynamic column qualifier:

Row keyrisk:1Yrisk:2Yrisk:3Yrisk:4Yrisk:TOTAL
POS-TRD-000123|20260731|EOD+650+1,900+10,400+1,550+14,500
  • The twist: that wide row is only the logical view. Physically HBase writes every cell as its own key-value entry — (row key, column qualifier, timestamp) → value. There is no array on disk. The qualifier 1Y is Term Node wearing a disguise; a vega qualifier like 6M|1.38 is Term Node 1 and Term Node 2 concatenated. HBase has been quietly running the one-row-per-number model all along — it just stores the describing attribute inside the column name instead of a named column.
  • The real migration risk: the semantics live in undocumented naming conventions — is it 1Y, 01Y or 1.0Y? how exactly does a vega qualifier concatenate expiry and strike? which column family means which metric family? The qualifier dictionary from the producing team is the mapping spec, and slots directly into the producer-mapping action item from §15.
  • Cell timestamps ≈ a lossy inserted-time: HBase keeps N versions per cell by timestamp — a partial, retention-limited cousin of the two clocks. The CDM's Record Effective/Inserted Time is the upgrade, not a duplication.

Costume 2 — JSON in flight: arrays are allowed here

For data movement, packaging the vector as a nested list is correct and efficient — one message per position per run:

{ "positionId": "POS-TRD-000123", "positionDate": "2026-07-31", "snapType": "EOD",
  "positionVersion": "V1", "runId": "EOD-AMER-20260731-R1", "runType": "OFFICIAL_EOD",
  "metrics": [
    { "metricType": "IR_DELTA", "riskFactor": "USD-SOFR-OIS", "termNode": "1Y", "value": 650, "ccy": "USD" },
    { "metricType": "IR_DELTA", "riskFactor": "USD-SOFR-OIS", "termNode": "2Y", "value": 1900, "ccy": "USD" },
    { "metricType": "IR_DELTA", "riskFactor": "USD-SOFR-OIS", "termNode": "3Y", "value": 10400, "ccy": "USD" }, "…" ] }

The array exists in the JSON without existing in the model: it's a serialization convenience, exploded back to one row per element on landing (an "unpivot"). Grain is the invariant; packaging is free to differ. Note the shared header carries the key + run once — which is also the natural place the completeness contract rides ("this run, 5 elements").

Costume 3 — Databricks at rest: long rows in family tables

  • Shape: exactly the §14 boxes made physical — long-form rows, one per number. The "one big table vs several" question is a physical choice the logical model deliberately leaves open.
  • Why a small family split wins: valuations, sensitivities, cashflows and scenario results have wildly different volumes and access patterns (scenario rows can be 1000× valuation rows; an EOD PV query shouldn't scan a VaR batch). Pattern: three or four family tables sharing an identical key prefix (position id, system, date, snap, leg, version, run id, the two record times) plus their family columns (Term Node/Node 2, Scenario Id, Cashflow Date) — with a unifying view on top for "all metrics". This is also the same instinct as the meeting's leg-vs-trade-level table segregation (§12): make misuse hard in the physical layout.
  • If one big table is chosen anyway: workable — partition by date/snap, cluster by position + metric type, accept sparse nulls (cheap in Delta). Just harder to tune, and every consumer scans past families they never asked for.
  • Trap to flag: Delta time travel is not bitemporality. It's an operational undo bounded by VACUUM/retention — not a queryable business history. Record Effective/Inserted Time must be real columns with append-only merges, exactly as the model says; otherwise Part II's restatement story becomes unreproducible the day housekeeping runs.

Where the CDM actually sits — the Unity Catalog layer

The CDM is not one of the three stores; it is the agreement all three are held to, and Unity Catalog is where that agreement gets enforced rather than merely documented:

CDM elementUnity Catalog enforcement
The metric grain and attributes (§14 boxes)Table schemas + column contracts — a producer physically cannot land a row without the key, version, run id.
Governed vocabularies (Risk Factor, Term Node, Scenario names)Reference tables + constraint checks — the P&L-explain join (§11) stops depending on naming luck.
The controls (version handshake §10, run completeness §13)Data-quality expectations on ingest: VERSION_MISMATCH and INCOMPLETE_SET become nightly PASS/FAIL, not reconciliation surprises.
Domain ownership & reload-only correction (§15 operating model)Access policies + lineage: custodians own writes, consumers read views, and every restated number traces to its run.

So the stack reads: CDM owns meaning · JSON owns movement · HBase/Delta own storage · Unity Catalog holds everyone to the CDM. The HBase wide rows aren't a contradiction of the model — they're evidence for it, one parse away.

A

Appendix — the meeting in plain English

A narrative retelling of the three recordings for anyone who was in and out of the call. Names are as heard in the auto-transcription (approximate). §15 has the same content compressed into decisions and action items; this is the story version. Jargon decoded inline: VS = the valuation service producing PVs; Book of Record (BOR) = the source trading systems (Calypso etc.); SPRX = a downstream reporting platform (used in the call only as an example); TDVIP = the data platform the CDM tables live on; close-off = the thrice-daily "pencils down" event a system publishes, listing which trades and versions make up its snapshot.

What the meeting was for

Aniket opened with the goal: the platform is onboarding positions from the book-of-record systems, and now needs to plug in the valuations that go with them — first for the Cancels & Amends project (regulatory, ~February go-live), whose consumers (Eugene's side, plus finance/US-reporting applications) need to know when this data will be available. So the session had three jobs: recap how the model works, resolve the open worries from last time, and start converging on timelines.

Act 1 — the recap

Aniket restated the design in two sentences: trades stream all day; at agreed moments you take a photograph — that photograph is a position (trade-level, aggregated bonds, cash balances, deposits, lending facilities — the six shapes of Part I). Everything you then measure about a position — its value, its Greeks, its cashflows — lands in one common metric schema, and the only rule that really matters is that the metric's position ID must match the position's ID. "That's the crux of it."

Act 2 — the leg debate (most of recording 1)

The first open loop: some systems produce valuations per leg, not per trade — will the model cope? Chris and Aniket: yes, by design. The position record holds the trade's economics including both legs; each metric row says which leg it belongs to (Leg Type, and a Leg Identifier in the newer model version). A cross-currency swap therefore produces two valuation records — a USD one and a CAD one.

That answer is what triggered the real debate. Eugene's worry: fine, but most consumers need one number per trade. To get it they must convert currencies and add the legs up themselves — and different consumers are mandated different FX rates (an EU report must use the ECB rate; others use end-of-day). So two perfectly correct consumers will publish two different "official" PVs for the same trade, and then reg reporting, P&L and trade surveillance stop reconciling with each other. He was careful to frame it: the data model is fine; it's the operating model that scares him.

Three responses emerged, none final: (1) source the most granular truth — leg level, native currency — because any trade-level number bakes in someone's FX choice; (2) optionally stamp a standard CAD-equivalent on every record so simple consumers can just sum (this became §12 and §14's Reporting-Equivalent columns); (3) if a producer also ships its own trade-level number, label it so nobody adds legs and the total (the double-counting guard, ditto §12). A cousin problem surfaced alongside: some sources don't say which leg is which — no buy/sell stamp, just "leg 1 / leg 2" — and today a downstream platform does fragile matching to line trade legs up with valuation legs. The model speaks pay/receive; agreeing the producer-to-model leg mapping was parked for a separate session with the trade-domain owners.

Act 3 — the version handshake (recording 2, the sharpest six minutes)

Then the amends question, which is really the question for a Cancels & Amends project: what guarantees that the valuation was computed on the same version of the trade that the position snapshot shows? The failure mode spelled out in the call: the valuation service prices version 15, the CDM's curated pipeline dropped an event and its position says version 13 — and now "our reg is off, our trade surveillance is off," and nobody can tell by looking.

The resolution came in three steps. Aniket proposed the simple mechanic: put the version on the metric record, compare it nightly to the version the position cites, flag mismatches, investigate and reload. Eli agreed but pushed further — identifying breaks isn't preventing them; ideally the producer publishes exactly which records it valued (a manifest). That idea hit a wall everyone respected: it would quietly move ownership of the snapshot from the Book of Record to the valuation system, and nobody wanted that. Landing position: BOR owns the snap; every producer and the CDM anchor to the same close-off events (three per day); the version column makes agreement checkable. In practice versions should only diverge when the pipeline drops one — which is precisely what the check will catch. This is §10's version handshake, and it was the one model change with unanimous support.

Act 4 — will it stretch? (recording 3)

Robin joined late and usefully re-asked everything: was the risk-metric model reviewed, who transforms producer output into the CDM, and what about systems the valuation service doesn't cover? The model feedback to date was confirmed as just one item — carry the version / bitemporal coordinates from the source — plus the pay/receive leg point for reporting.

Then the forward-looking probes: today's need is PVs, but does the structure extend to credit delta (yes — it's a curve, fits now), Vega ("it's a grid, not a rate" — needs the second dimension, §13/§14's Term Node 2), and VaR/SVaR (scenario-based: apply a named shock, get a whole set of PVs — needs a scenario identifier, and an open choice of storing scenario PVs vs P&Ls). Nobody tried to settle these on the call; the comfort sought and given was that they're additions, not a rewrite.

A practical mapping gap surfaced here too: the valuation service's PV output carries only four fields — id, currency, metric name, value. Everything else on the metric record (book, region, etc.) must be enriched from the position side. Robin's team took the action to map their actual outputs to the CDM and bring back the gaps.

Act 5 — who owns it, and the clock on the wall

On ownership: the CDO side has a mapping framework — a mapping spreadsheet uploaded as configuration, executed by the standard pipeline — so onboarding a producer is mostly mapping work, not engineering. Per the domain-driven direction, the domain custodian owns the raw-to-CDM transformation and its controls (version match, missing values); crucially, nobody may edit a wrong number in place — the only correction is reloading the pipeline, which is the operating-model twin of the bitemporal design. Radhika raised the structural tension honestly: centralizing through a CDM costs time-to-market when producers invent new metrics and consumers need them "yesterday" — and noted Chetan is exploring a mesh/data-contract architecture in parallel. Agreed disposition: keep that conversation going, but it must not hold committed deliveries.

The closing stretch was scheduling with an edge to it. February is regulatory, daily valuation feeds must be flowing to the platform before go-live (historical backfill can follow), some sources are ready (Darwin, Compass produce CDM-shaped data), at least one isn't (Sophos), and — the moment of candor — several upstream system owners may not yet grasp that this is a regulatory dependency at all. The group agreed to sound the alarm now rather than at integration time: a standing cadence with the valuation-producing systems, escalations where needed, the consolidated backlog with Chris, and strict scope discipline — onboard what Cancels & Amends needs, not everything that exists.
